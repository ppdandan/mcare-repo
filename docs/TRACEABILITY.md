## The six check points in detail

**1. Is there any inventory feature in the database, the website, or the app?**
No. `git grep -il inventory` across `supabase/`, `web/`, `mobile/` returns nothing on either branch. There is no table, no migration, no page, no screen, no type in `shared/`. This part of Problems 1 and 4 and Objectives 2 and 3 is entirely absent.

**2. Can staff register a walk-in visit, for a patient with or without an app account? Does it show on the calendar and in analytics?**
Half of it works. Staff can register a walk-in **patient** without an app account (`web/src/app/dashboard/patients/page.tsx:76` — `supabase.auth.signUp` with a generated temp password, confirmed at `:99` and the "Register Walk-in Patient" modal at `:443`). But there is **no flow anywhere on the web to create a walk-in appointment**: `grep` for inserts into `appointments` across `web/src` returns nothing — the appointments page only updates statuses. So a walk-in visit only exists if the patient's account books it from the phone. The database side is ready (the insert policy at `00007_rls_policies.sql:270` already allows staff inserts), so this is a UI-level gap, not a schema one. Anything that does get created — by mobile booking or by a future staff form — shows on the calendar and in analytics automatically, since both count all appointments regardless of origin.

**3. Is there a clinic information or location screen on mobile and on the website?**
No, on both counts. On mobile the clinic's address appears only as incidental static text (`welcome.tsx:30`, `index.tsx:114`), and booking surfaces clinic hours only as an empty state (`book.tsx:391`). There is no clinic-info screen, no map, and no display of the clinic's phone or email. On the web the root page redirects straight to `/login` (`web/src/app/page.tsx:8`); the only public pages are privacy, delete-account, and the patient-app notice — no public clinic page. Objective 4 is therefore mostly unmet.

**4. Which analytics exist today? Do they cover demographics, inventory consumption, and payment summaries?**
Existing (`web/src/app/dashboard/analytics/page.tsx`): patient registrations over time (BarChart, `:342`), appointment trends (AreaChart, `:364`), status distribution (PieChart, `:405`), top services (BarChart, `:445`), busiest days/hours, and top-5 diagnoses aggregated from medical records — all with 7d/30d/90d/all range filters. Coverage against the thesis: appointment trends — covered; demographics — **not** (registrations ≠ demographics; there is no age breakdown and the database has no sex column at all); inventory consumption — **not** (nothing to analyze); payment summaries — **partially**: the Reports page's summary CSV (`reports/page.tsx:16,62-78`, `exportSummaryCsv`) totals transaction count, not-voided amount, and voided count per date range, but the Analytics page itself has no payment chart.

**5. Can staff find a returning patient and open their full history across years?**
Yes. Patients page search (`patients/page.tsx:11,179,239`) finds the returning patient; the records page fetches medical records without a date cap and filters by patient name / physician / diagnosis and sorts by visit date or patient (`records/page.tsx:216-223`), so history across years is reachable in one place. The records themselves are permanent in the DB (no destructive flow; `00022` adds only soft-delete + a documented purge helper). Caveat: no test was run in this audit to demonstrate a multi-year retrieval end-to-end; the flow is clear from code.

**6. Can a patient see only the records the clinic has authorized them to see?**
Yes, enforced twice. RLS `medical_records_select_policy` (`00007_rls_policies.sql`) permits a patient row only when `patient_id = current_patient_id()`, otherwise only clinical staff. The mobile app additionally filters its own fetch to the signed-in patient and marks each open with an audit event (`records.tsx` → `log_medical_record_access` RPC, `00008_audit_trigger.sql`), which is the DPA 2012 / RA 10173 evidence trail. Staff-side visibility is limited to clinical roles (doctor/nurse/admin), not receptionists.
